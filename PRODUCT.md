# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, founders, and random visitors land here on a laptop or phone and want to understand who you are, what you have built, and why you are worth remembering. They are not here to browse a dense case-study archive, they are here for a fast, personal introduction with enough proof to feel real.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio presents you as a creative student-builder with taste, momentum, and real shipped work. It should make the first impression feel personal and distinctive, then quickly surface selected projects, contact paths, and a sense of how you think.

Success means a visitor feels that the site is clearly yours, not a generic CS template, and can leave with a strong memory of your personality and capability.

## Brand Personality

Personal, sharp, builder-energy, slightly playful, confident.

The voice should feel like a creative student who ships, not a corporate bio. It should be warm enough to feel human, precise enough to feel credible, and bold enough to stand apart.

## Anti-references

Generic student portfolio templates, AI-sloppy layouts, sterile SaaS gradients, default hero-metric dashboards, overused editorial-serif landing pages, and copy that sounds like a LinkedIn summary.

Avoid looking like a clone of the reference sites. Borrow their feeling of care, pacing, and personalization, but not their exact visual language or structure.

## Design Principles

1. Put personality in the first fold, so the site feels authored within seconds.
2. Show proof early, with projects and outcomes that feel concrete and selective.
3. Make the contrast part of the story, especially between delicate cherry blossom softness and Ferrari-level energy.
4. Keep the structure simple enough for mobile, but not so simple that it feels templated.
5. Use copy and composition to signal taste, confidence, and momentum without sounding inflated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard accessibility. Keep motion subtle and provide reduced-motion handling. Make the site readable on small screens, and do not rely on color alone to convey meaning.
